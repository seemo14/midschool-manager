1AC: My FamilyUnit 2 · Family members + possessive adjectives

Objectives

  • Name 10+ family members
  • Use my / your / his / her correctly
  • Introduce a family member in 2–3 sentences

Materials

  • Board, flashcards or a drawn family tree, A4 sheets

Stages

1. Warm-up 5′ Draw your own simple family tree on the board (or a made-up one). Ask: "Who is this?" and let students guess.
2. Vocabulary 12′ Teach: father, mother, brother, sister, grandfather, grandmother, uncle, aunt, cousin, son, daughter, parents. Drill in chorus, then individually. Check with gender pairs (brother ↔ sister).
3. Grammar: possessive adjectives 10′
I → my You → your He → his She → her This is MY brother. HIS name is Adam.
Gap-fill on the board (5 sentences). Students come up to the board and correct it.
4. Practice (pairs) 12′ Each student draws their family tree. In pairs: "Who's this?" – "This is my uncle. His name is …"
5. Production 12′ Write 4 sentences: "My father is … His name is … He is … years old." 3–4 volunteers read theirs aloud.
6. Wrap-up 4′ Exit ticket: 3 quick oral questions (e.g. "Your father's brother is your …?").

Assessment

  • Exit-ticket answers and the written sentences

Homework

  • Bring a family photo or drawing with 5 sentences under it

Differentiation

  • Weaker students: sentence frames on the board · Stronger students: add jobs and ages
2AC: Daily RoutinesUnit 2 · Present simple + adverbs of frequency

Objectives

  • Describe a daily routine in the present simple
  • Place adverbs of frequency correctly
  • Use 3rd-person -s accurately

Materials

  • Board, 6 routine picture prompts (drawn or printed), survey grid

Stages

1. Warm-up 5′ Ask: "What time do you get up?" Students answer and you note the times on the board.
2. Vocabulary review 8′ wake up, get up, have breakfast, go to school, have lunch, do homework, watch TV, go to bed. Put the pictures in order.
3. Grammar 12′
0% never – sometimes – often – usually – always 100% I ALWAYS get up at 7. She USUALLY goes to school by bus. He is NEVER late. (after "be")
Elicit the 3rd-person -s rule and do 6 quick transformation sentences.
4. Practice: survey 12′ Grid of 6 activities. Students ask 3 classmates: "How often do you …?"
5. Production 13′ Report a partner's routine in writing (6 sentences, 3rd person): "Sara usually gets up at 6:30…"
6. Wrap-up 5′ Error hunt: 4 incorrect sentences on the board for the class to fix.

Assessment

  • Survey reports, checking the -s ending and adverb position

Homework

  • Write about your weekend routine (8 sentences, 4 adverbs)

Differentiation

  • Weaker students: a word bank · Stronger students: add time linkers (first, then, after that)
3AC: Past EventsUnit 2 · Past simple (regular / irregular)

Objectives

  • Form the past simple: affirmative, negative, questions
  • Use 10 common irregular verbs
  • Narrate a past event in a short paragraph

Materials

  • Board, irregular-verb list, short model text (below)

Stages

1. Warm-up 5′ "What did you do last weekend?" Collect the answers and underline the verbs.
2. Reading 10′
Last summer, Yassine visited his grandparents in Ifrane. He travelled by bus. He didn't stay long, but he went hiking and ate tagine every day. Did he like it? Yes, he loved it!
Comprehension: 3 questions, then find all the past verbs.
3. Grammar 12′
+ He visited / went – He didn't visit / didn't go ? Did he visit? – Yes, he did. Irregular: go-went, eat-ate, see-saw, have-had, buy-bought, take-took, make-made, come-came, get-got, write-wrote
Pronouncing -ed: /t/ walked, /d/ played, /ɪd/ visited.
4. Practice 12′ Find Someone Who: "Did you travel last summer?" 6 prompts, and students write down names.
5. Production 12′ Paragraph of 60–80 words: "My best day last summer", using 5 irregular verbs.
6. Wrap-up 4′ Quick verb quiz in chorus (base → past) with the 10 verbs.

Assessment

  • Paragraph check: verb forms and negatives

Homework

  • Learn the 10 irregular verbs (quiz next session) and finish the paragraph

Differentiation

  • Weaker students: a paragraph frame · Stronger students: add linkers (then, after that, finally)
